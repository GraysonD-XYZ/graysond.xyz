Crypto was an early mass-market example of the overlay problem: complex financial, technical, legal, and custody systems were made accessible through clean consumer interfaces that often created more trust than the underlying mechanics justified.

Users saw balances, charts, rewards, and buy/sell buttons while absorbing risks related to custody, liquidity, platform solvency, tax treatment, and governance. The central lesson is not only that speculative assets are risky; it is that modern platforms can make complex systems usable before they are understandable. The interface simplifies the action, not necessarily the risk.

This essay builds on existing work around dark patterns, notice-and-consent failure, digital identity, crypto investor protection, and platform governance. Its contribution is the overlay framework: a practical way to describe how simple interfaces and seamless platform fabrics can preserve user trust while hiding system depth, boundary-crossing, and risk transfer.
